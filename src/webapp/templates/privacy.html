Relic Solver Privacy

The short version

No cookies. No accounts. No advertising. No analytics or tracking scripts of any kind. Nothing you type here is tied to you.

The site is served through Cloudflare, who keep ordinary traffic records the way every network in between does. Details below.

What the site receives

Pressing Run Solver sends the numbers you entered - chest counts, the relic cap and your targets - to the server, which calculates the odds and sends them back. They are game numbers, not personal information.

Those numbers are kept in a results cache so that an identical question is answered instantly next time. The cache is keyed on the numbers alone. It records nothing about who asked.

A usage record is also kept: one row per calculation, holding the numbers you entered, the resulting probability, and how long the calculation took. It exists so the solver can be checked against what people actually ask it, and it is capped at the most recent 50,000 rows. The row has no address, no identifier and no way to be tied back to a visitor or to any other row.

The solver uses your IP address for one thing: a counter that limits how many requests one address may make, so a single visitor cannot take it down for everyone. The counter holds a tally and expires on its own. It is never logged and never stored beside the numbers you entered.

The server's own logs record what it did - how long a calculation took, and any errors - and carry no IP address, no browser details and none of the numbers you entered.

What stays in your browser

Your last inputs are saved in your browser's local storage so the form is still filled in when you come back. That never leaves your device. The entry is dropped on its own after 90 days, and clearing your browser data removes it sooner.

Other parties

Nothing on this page is fetched from another origin. Styles, scripts, fonts and images all come from this site, including the Ko-fi button in the footer, so no third party sees a request simply because you opened the page. The links to GitHub, Discord and Ko-fi are ordinary links: they reach those sites only when you follow them.

The site runs on Heroku, whose routing layer keeps its own short-lived access logs, as any web host does.

Since October 2026 the pages are delivered through Cloudflare, which serves them from somewhere near you and absorbs traffic floods. Requests reach them first, so they see what any network on the way sees: the address it came from, the page asked for and which browser asked.
